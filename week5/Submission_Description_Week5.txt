I am submitting "Model Evaluation and Reporting Strategy for Agribusiness Forecasting," a 48-page evaluation report (19,861 words, 40 tables, 12 figures) that answers a question the modelling work cannot answer on its own: what may an agribusiness forecast legitimately be used for?

Week 5 extends the Week 1 analysis plan, the Week 2 data contracts, the Week 3 indicator design and the Week 4 six-rung forecasting framework. It defines a decision-grade metric hierarchy covering MAE, RMSE, MASE, R-squared, bias, pinball loss, interval coverage, the Winkler score, directional accuracy and cost-weighted error, then explains where each metric misleads. The design is a 60-origin rolling backtest on real World Bank Pink Sheet prices from January 2000 to October 2026, with four horizons, six models and 1,440 fold-level forecasts.

The executed audit is deliberately unglamorous. The ensemble passes the accuracy gate at one, six and twelve months but fails at three months. The 2.1% one-month edge has a paired 95% interval of -1.30 to +0.75 USD/MT (p = 0.615), so no improvement is statistically separable from zero. Directional accuracy is 46.7–55.0%, inside the noise band around chance, and the twelve-month interval covers 78.8% of outcomes against a nominal 90%. A cross-commodity check clears the three-month gate for maize, soybeans and cotton but not for wheat or rice. The report reconciles these results with Week 4's design targets rather than hiding the gap, and shows why a design-target MASE of 0.54 and a measured 0.979 are a specification versus an outcome, not a coding error.

The reporting system translates the evidence for non-specialists: a fan chart, a benchmark scorecard, calibration curves, a one-screen dashboard, a four-box executive summary and a limitation register. It concludes with a prioritised twelve-month roadmap and a clear recommendation: use the model as a planning range at one, six and twelve months, publish the random walk where the gate fails, and do not present it as a directional trading or daily timing signal.

The complete code, executed data and reproducible build are available at https://github.com/SamrudhSS/yuvaintern.
