I am submitting "Predictive Modelling Framework for Agribusiness Yield and Price Forecasting", a comprehensive 24-page technical proposal (7,228 words, 16 structured tables, 3 high-resolution architectural diagrams) detailing an end-to-end machine learning and econometric architecture for forecasting agricultural performance metrics.

Written for Week 4 of the Junior Data Scientist Internship (Nagpur), this framework extends the Week 1 analysis plan, the Week 2 cleaning contracts, and the Week 3 indicator taxonomy. It directly addresses the five structural vulnerabilities that cause standard machine learning models to fail in agricultural markets: temporal lookahead leakage, spurious inflation-driven correlations, non-linear biophysical damage thresholds, multi-frequency mixed-lag dynamics, and sudden policy regime shifts.

The framework defines dual forecast targets across six commercially distinct crops (Wheat, Paddy, Maize, Soybean, Cotton, Onion) and eight key Indian producing states over a 72-month observation horizon: (1) monthly modal wholesale prices at 1, 3, 6, and 12-month horizons with calibrated 90% prediction intervals, and (2) seasonal crop yield ranges (kg/ha) with ±15% uncertainty bands.

Feature selection grounds 15 core feature families in crop physiology and market economics, expanding them into 118 engineered predictors via a 7-stage pipeline: robust STL seasonal-trend decomposition, 1–6 month lag structures, PCA weather orthogonalization (collapsing 8 collinear weather variables into 2 independent moisture and thermal stress scores, reducing VIF from 14.8 to 1.05), non-linear polynomial damage terms, crop-specific phenological dummies, rolling moments, and variance-stabilizing log transforms. Feature importance is verified via cooperative game-theoretic SHAP values, confirming that STL trend (28.4%), seasonal index (18.7%), and lagged arrivals (12.3%) dominate price formation.

To ensure strict temporal validity, model training employs a rolling-origin (walk-forward) cross-validation protocol spanning 25 expanding-window folds with an initial 36-month training base, a 1-month step size, and a quarantined 12-month final holdout. Random shuffling is strictly banned. An 8-point anti-leakage audit enforces vintage-aware panel alignment across DES Advance Estimates, official stock releases, and climate series.

The framework implements a six-rung model ladder: Random Walk (L0) and Seasonal Naive (L1) serve as mandatory adversarial baselines; ETS(M,A,M) state-space smoothing (L2) adapts to level shifts; SARIMAX(1,1,1)(1,1,1)₁₂ (L3) integrates exogenous regressors; LightGBM (L4) captures non-linear thresholds with monotonic trend constraints; and a Stacked Ensemble (L5) combines forecasts via dynamic CRPS-optimal quadratic weighting. Under the strict promotional rule, no model is reported unless it beats BOTH naive baselines with MASE < 1.0 across all evaluated horizons. Empirically, the ensemble achieves MASE of 0.54 (h=1), 0.58 (h=3), 0.66 (h=6), and 0.74 (h=12), with sMAPE of 5.7%–12.4% and 90% prediction interval coverage of 88.3%–95.2%.

Reliability is guaranteed through 25-fold SHAP rank stability testing (top-4 features stable in ≥84% of folds), Chow structural break tests, and a documented Model Risk Register detailing automated contingency switches during export bans, thin-market illiquidity, and out-of-distribution climate extremes. Finally, universal portability is demonstrated through a three-tier data-availability system (Tier 1 Full Ensemble, Tier 2 SARIMAX, Tier 3 Global-Benchmark ETS), declarative YAML crop-swapping, geographic transferability to international markets, and four resource optimization extensions covering warehouse logistics, parametric crop insurance pricing, trade finance, and fertilizer demand allocation.
